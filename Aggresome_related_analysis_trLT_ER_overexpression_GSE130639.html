1 Aim and data source

The aim of this script is to analyze bulk RNA-seq data from human embryonic fibroblasts with Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) truncated large tumor antigen (TLT), early region (ER), and GFP control inducible vectors. We will look for genes and pathways associated with proteostasis, particularly with interest in aggresome, unfolded protein response, and autophagy using annotated gene sets. The data was downloaded from the GEO database, series GSE130639. The data is described in more detail in Yang et al., 2025 (doi: 10.1172/JCI177724).

We will focus on samples at 24 and 48 hours after induction of TLT, ER, or the control vectors.

2 Summary of main findings

In the following analysis we show these main findings:

  • UPR response, as defined by Hallmark gene sets, is upregulated in ER vs. GFP (Figure 1) and downregulated in TLT vs. GFP (Figure 3), however the running enrichment score shows these enrichments to be bidirectional, with gene hits at both extremes (Figure 2, Figure 4).

  • Enriched proteostasis-related GO terms showed upregulation of chaperones and their binding, but a decrease in autophagy (Figure 7, Figure 8). Similar patterns were observed for proteostasis-related Reactome terms (Figure 5, Figure 6).

  • Top-ranked genes in the select proteostasis-related pathways mostly shared expression patterns across ER and TLT replicates (Figure 11). The shown pathways were selected to be minimally overlapping (Figure 9, Figure 10) to broadly cover most of the enriched terms in both comparisons.

  • Aggresome-specific signature was positively associated to TLT or ER expression, but not significant (Figure 12, Figure 13).

  • Although not all samples at 48hrs post induction were available, the available samples show the same trends in proteostasis pathways as the 24hr-timepoint highlighted in the results above.

4.1 DESeq2 for normalization and DEG

Next, DESeq2 is used for data normalization and differential gene expression analysis.

Conditions are defined.

Show code 
library("DESeq2")

metadata <- data.frame(condition = factor(gsub("_24_(1|2|3)", "", colnames(count_24))), 
                      replicate = factor(gsub("[A-Za]+_24_", "", colnames(count_24)))) 
rownames(metadata) <- colnames(count_24)

dds <- DESeqDataSetFromMatrix(countData = as.matrix(count_24),
                              colData = metadata,
                              design = ~ condition)

Low-expressed genes are filtered and the model is fitted.

Show code 
# Remove genes have less than 3 samples with 10 counts or more
keep <- rowSums(counts(dds) >= 10) >= 3
dds <- dds[keep,]

#Deseq function automatically estimates size factors and dispersions
dds <- DESeq(dds)

Additionally, data is normalized and transformed for eventual heatmap plotting.
